What the two adders are actually doing

Each 74HC283 is not one full adder. It is four full adders in one package, chained internally, so it adds two 4-bit numbers in one shot. You have two of those packages, U1 and U2, doing two different jobs.

U1 — the plain binary sum

U1 knows nothing about BCD. It adds A and B as ordinary binary and hands you a 5-bit answer. Four of those bits come out of the sum pins; the fifth is the carry.

Read them together as one number: K·16 + Z3·8 + Z2·4 + Z1·2 + Z0·1. Two BCD digits can only reach 9 + 9, so the range is 0 to 18.

The sums are not a decimal digit. For answers 0 through 9, binary and BCD happen to look identical, so it feels like it is working. From 10 up they split apart. That split is the entire reason the rest of the circuit exists.

Why all four Z lines get wired

Because all four are part of the answer. Leave one open and you have thrown away a power of two. They do not all go to the same places, though.

How C gets decided

C = K + Z3·Z2 + Z3·Z1

C asks one question: did the answer go past 9? There are two ways it can, and they need separate detection.

The K term. K = 1 means the sum reached 16 or more. The most you can ever get is 18, so K = 1 means 16, 17 or 18. All three are past 9, so K alone is proof.

The gate terms catch 10 through 15, where K is still 0. You cannot just use Z3: Z3 is high for 8 through 15, and 8 and 9 are perfectly valid. So you qualify it. Z3·Z2 catches 12–15, Z3·Z1 catches 10, 11, 14, 15. Together they cover 10–15 and leave 8 (1000) and 9 (1001) alone, because both of those have Z2 and Z1 at 0.

The trap in 9 + 9

9 + 9 = 18. In binary that is 10010: K = 1 and Z3..Z0 = 0010. The sum pins read 2. They look like a perfectly valid BCD digit. The AND gates see Z3 = 0 and report nothing wrong.

Only K knows. This is why the carry term is not optional, and why slide 10's Err = S3S2 + S3S1 is incomplete on its own — the Cy term gets folded back in on slide 11.

U2 — add six, or add nothing

Binary rolls over at 16. Decimal rolls over at 10. The gap is 6. When the answer should have carried into the tens but binary did not, adding 6 forces the rollover and drops the remainder exactly where it belongs.

U2's A inputs are Z3..Z0. Its B inputs are strapped 0 C C 0, which is 0110 = 6 when C is high and 0000 = 0 when C is low. One signal, no extra gates. U2 computes Z + 6C, and S3..S0 is the ones digit, always 0 through 9.

U2 pin 9 is left open on purpose. When correction fires, Z + 6 often overflows past 15 and U2 raises its own carry. That carry says the same thing C already said, and C is the tens digit driving the LED. Wiring it would add nothing.

How the circuit works

U1 adds the two operands as plain binary. If the answer is bigger than 9 it is not valid BCD, so four gates raise a signal called C, and U2 adds 6 to fix it.

C = K + Z3·Z2 + Z3·Z1

K is U1's carry out, pin 9. Z3..Z0 is U1's sum. C is also the tens digit, so it drives the LED. U2's carry out is not used at all.

U2's B inputs are wired 0 C C 0. That feeds it 0110 (six) when C is high and 0000 when C is low. That one trick is the whole correction.

If something is wrong

Display is blank

Check CD4511 pin 4 (/BL) is at +5 V and pin 5 (LE) is at ground. Floating CMOS pins are the usual cause. A blank display can also mean an invalid code reached the decoder, which means the correction logic failed.

Display shows nothing but the digit is right on the adder

Wrong display polarity. The CD4511 needs a common cathode part.

LED lights on 7 + 2 = 9

The AND gates are on the wrong bits. They must be Z3 with Z2, and Z3 with Z1. Not Z1 and Z0.

Digits flicker or read wrong

If the adders are 74LS, not 74HC, the CD4511 cannot read their high level. Add 4.7 kΩ pull-ups to +5 V on S3..S0.

Everything is dead

Check the rail bridges. The top and bottom rails on a breadboard are not connected to each other unless you jumper them.
